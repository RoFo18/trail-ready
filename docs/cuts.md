# Safety facts corrected

- Nail puncture care. Was: Let it bleed a little, wash, bandage. Now: Stop any bleeding with gentle pressure, rinse it clean, cover it, see a doctor about tetanus. Source: Mayo Clinic, Puncture wounds: First aid (mayoclinic.org/first-aid/first-aid-puncture-wounds/basics/art-20056665).
- Burn cooling time. Was: Cool with cool water for at least 10 minutes. Now: Cool under cool running water for 15 to 20 minutes, no ice. Source: American Red Cross, Burns: How to Help (redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/burns).
- CPR for untrained rescuers. Was: Start CPR if trained. Now: Not trained? Hands-only CPR: push hard and fast in the center of the chest. Source: American Heart Association, Hands-Only CPR (heart.org).
- Severe bleeding. Was: Direct pressure only. Now: Direct pressure; a trained person adds a tourniquet for life-threatening arm or leg bleeding. Source: 2024 AHA and American Red Cross Guidelines for First Aid (professional.heart.org).
- Sea lion distance. Was: At least 50 yards. Now: Stay well back; never approach (number dropped as trivia). Source: NOAA Fisheries marine mammal viewing guidelines.

- Hypothermia. Was: Medical help listed last. Now: Severe signs (confused, stumbling, stopped shivering): call 911 first. Source: American Red Cross, Hypothermia (redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/hypothermia).
- CPR after drowning. Was: Compressions only, in a water rescue scenario. Now: General collapse scenario; drowning note: trained rescuers give breaths with compressions. Source: American Heart Association, CPR with rescue breaths vital after drowning, Nov 2024 (heart.org).

- Shock position. Was: raise the feet if it doesn't hurt. Now: raise the feet unless you suspect a head, neck, back or leg injury. Source: American Red Cross, Shock (redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/shock); Scouts BSA Handbook.
