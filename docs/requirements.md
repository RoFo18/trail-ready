# Second Class requirements covered by the app

Source: Scouting America, Second Class rank requirements (current version),
https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Second-Class-v2.pdf
Requirement text below is a close summary of the official wording; the PDF governs.

Each entry lists what a leader expects the Scout to say or do at sign-off. That list is the only
reason any content may exist in the app. App key in brackets.

## 1b Recite the Leave No Trace principles from memory; explain how you follow them [lnt]
- Say all seven principles. Order not required.
- Give a concrete example of following each on an outing.

## 1c Choose a patrol site; explain what to consider in choosing a site and tent location [campsite]
- Permitted / allowed to camp there.
- Safety: no dead limbs overhead (widowmakers), not in a dry wash or low spot that floods, away from cliff edges and hazards.
- Durable, level surface; Leave No Trace (established sites, 200 ft from water).
- Water, shelter from wind, drainage; comfort comes after safety.

## 2a When a cooking or other fire is appropriate and when it isn't [whenfire]
- Appropriate: rules allow it, existing fire ring, enough dead and down wood, low fire danger, a real need.
- Not appropriate: fire restrictions or red-flag conditions, high wind, dry conditions, no fire ring, LNT area, a stove would do.

## 2b Use a pocketknife (and saw or axe if needed) to prepare tinder, kindling and fuel wood [firewood]
- Recognize the three sizes and what each does.
- Use the knife safely: cut away from yourself, blood circle, close it when not in use.
- Gather dead and down wood only.

## 2c Build a minimal-impact fire, burn 2+ minutes, extinguish, dispose of ashes [buildfire]
- Use an existing ring or fire pan; clear area; water and shovel ready before lighting.
- Tinder, then kindling, then fuel; know a fire lay.
- Extinguish: drown, stir, drown until cold to the touch.
- Ashes: cold, then handled per site rules (scatter or pack out).

## 2d When to use a lightweight or propane stove; set up, light, safety [stoves]
- Use a stove when fires aren't allowed, are restricted, or to reduce impact; quick cooking.
- Safety: set up on a level, stable surface outdoors, away from tents and fuel; light the match before opening the valve; never use in a tent; let it cool; check connections for leaks.

## 2f / 2g Tie a sheet bend and a bowline; describe a situation for each [knots]
- Sheet bend: joining two ropes, including different sizes.
- Bowline: a fixed loop that won't slip, e.g. a rescue loop or tying to a post.
- Recognize each knot.

## 3a How a compass works; orient a map; five map symbols [compass]
- Needle points to magnetic north; parts of the compass.
- Orient a map with a compass (and declination where relevant), or by terrain.
- Identify five common map symbols.

## 3b Compass and map hike (hands-on) [compass]
- Core knowledge for the day: take and follow a bearing.

## 3c Hazards or injuries on the hike and how to prevent them [hazards]
- Weather, heat and dehydration, blisters, sprains and falls, getting lost, wildlife and plants, water crossings, darkness.

## 3d Find directions by day and night without a compass [nocompass]
- Day: shadow-stick method; sun rises east, sets west.
- Night: North Star via the Big Dipper pointer stars.

## 4 Identify or show evidence of 10 kinds of wild animals in your area [animals, region file]
- Recognize ten animals likely in the Scout's area, by sight or sign, and keep a safe distance.

## 5a Precautions for a safe swim (Safe Swim Defense) [safeswim]
- Know the eight points and what each means at a swim.

## 5c Reaching, object and throwing-line rescues [rescue]
- Reach: get low and braced; extend an object; pull in.
- Throw: throw a line or floating object past the person and pull them in.

## 5d Why reach/throw before swimming; how a rescue swimmer avoids contact [noswim]
- Reach, throw, row, go. A panicked person can pull a rescuer under.
- Rescue swimmer pushes a float to the victim, stays out of reach.

## 6a First aid for: object in the eye, warm-blooded animal bites, puncture wounds, second-degree burns, heat exhaustion, shock, heatstroke, dehydration, hypothermia, hyperventilation [firstaid]
- The correct first aid for each, as taught in the current Scouts BSA Handbook.

## 6b Stopped breathing, stroke, severe bleeding, ingested poisoning [hurry]
- Stopped breathing: call 911, AED, CPR (hands-only if untrained).
- Stroke: recognize the signs (face, arm, speech), note the time, call 911.
- Severe bleeding: firm direct pressure; tourniquet when trained and pressure fails.
- Ingested poison: call Poison Control or 911; don't induce vomiting unless told; save the container.

## 6c Prevent the injuries in 6a and 6b on a campout or hike [prevent]

## 6d Home and backcountry emergencies; what is an emergency; what to tell responders [emergency]
- Scene safety; call for help; location, what happened, number hurt, conditions, care given.
- Backcountry: stay with the patient, send two people for help with written info.

## 6e Responding to a vehicle accident [vehicle]
- Stay safe and off the road, call 911, warn traffic, don't move victims unless in danger, give first aid.

## 8a Take part in a flag ceremony [ceremony]
- Core knowledge for the day: roles, commands and steps for raising, lowering and posting colors.

## 8b Respect due the U.S. flag [flag]
- Salute and stand; flag never touches the ground; raised briskly, lowered slowly; half-staff procedure; folding; retiring a worn flag; display rules.

## 9a The three R's of personal safety [threer]
- Recognize, Resist, Report.

## 9b Bullying and the right response [bullying]
- What bullying is; how to respond for yourself and others; tell a trusted adult.

## Exception
- One question: the Pledge begins "I pledge allegiance," with no "of."

## Not covered (no knowledge content to study)
1a, 2e, 5b, 7a-7c, 8c-8e, 10-12.
